# Aspect / Facet Taxonomy

Version: 0.1 Draft  
Status: Staged for Review  
Scope: Tessera Tabletop Card System

---

# Purpose

This document defines the current working Aspect to Facet taxonomy for the Tessera card-based personality identity mapping system.

The taxonomy is designed to minimize top-level categories while preserving enough facet-level precision to support later expression libraries.

This document only governs the personality card system. The Karma model operates as a separate evaluative overlay and should not be merged into this taxonomy.

---

# Structural Principle

The Tessera personality hierarchy currently follows:

```text
Domain
↓
Aspect
↓
Facet
↓
Expression
↓
Reconciliation
```

Domains are organizational anchors.

Aspects are broad personality operating systems.

Facets are narrower tendency channels that generate expression cards.

Expressions are observable manifestations of facets.

Reconciliation explains discrepancies between assessment results, self-concept, observer perception, and behavioral evidence.

---

# Aspect Design Rule

An Aspect should be broad enough to contain multiple distinct facets and useful enough to organize a meaningful portion of the identity map.

An Aspect should not be duplicated as one of its own facets.

---

# Facet Design Rule

A Facet should:

- Represent a distinct psychological tendency.
- Generate multiple observable expressions.
- Avoid duplicating another facet.
- Remain narrower than its parent aspect.
- Improve identity-map precision.

---

# Current Aspect Set

The current taxonomy contains eight aspects:

1. Agency
2. Social Vitality
3. Communion
4. Self-Regulation
5. Emotional Stability
6. Exploration
7. Honesty
8. Reflection

---

# 1. Agency

## Core Theme

Influence, initiative, effectiveness, goal pursuit, and directed action.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Assertiveness | Expressing will, position, or preference directly. |
| Leadership | Coordinating people, directing collective activity, or organizing group effort. |
| Initiative | Beginning action without external prompting. |
| Influence | Affecting others' decisions, attention, behavior, or direction. |
| Self-Efficacy | Belief in one's capacity to act effectively and solve problems. |
| Achievement Orientation | Desire for accomplishment, advancement, mastery, or excellence. |
| Courage | Willingness to act despite fear, risk, or intimidation. |
| Visibility / Presence | Comfort being noticed, seen, or occupying social attention. |
| Perseverance | Continuing effort despite obstacles, fatigue, or resistance. |

## Notes

Social Confidence has been removed from Agency because it more accurately describes sustained social capacity than agentic force.

---

# 2. Social Vitality

## Core Theme

Social energy, interpersonal activation, expressive positivity, and the capacity to sustain engagement in dynamic social environments.

Social Vitality functions like a social energy reserve. It reflects how much social charge a person can bring into interaction before needing recovery or retreat.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sociability | Desire for and enjoyment of social company. |
| Enthusiasm | Expressive excitement, interest, and positive activation. |
| Humor | Tendency to perceive, create, or share amusement. |
| Playfulness | Lightness, spontaneity, and enjoyment in interaction. |
| Positive Affect | General tendency toward cheerfulness, optimism, and upbeat mood. |
| Affability | Ease, pleasantness, and approachability during interaction. |
| Personal Disclosure | Willingness to share personal thoughts, feelings, or experiences. |
| Social Participation | Frequency and willingness to join social activity. |
| Social Stamina | Capacity to remain socially engaged in large, demanding, or dynamic social environments without rapid depletion. |

## Notes

Warmth has been moved from Social Vitality to Communion because it describes relational receptivity more than social energy.

Hedonism has been removed from this taxonomy because it is not sufficiently specific to social vitality and may introduce value/pleasure-seeking ambiguity.

---

# 3. Communion

## Core Theme

Relational orientation, interpersonal concern, affiliative tendency, and natural responsiveness to others.

Communion is not equivalent to virtue. It describes how a person tends to relate to others. Karma evaluates the ethical direction and quality of that relational behavior.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Compassion | Sensitivity to others' suffering and inclination toward care. |
| Trust | Willingness to rely on, believe in, or extend good faith toward others. |
| Cooperation | Preference for shared effort, coordination, and mutual participation. |
| Protectiveness | Tendency to defend, safeguard, or look after others. |
| Warmth | Affectionate, welcoming, emotionally receptive interpersonal style. |

## Notes

Empathy, Altruism, Fairness, Forgiveness, Gratitude, and similar moralized constructs are not currently treated as Communion facets. These are better assessed through the Karma model where their ethical components can be evaluated with greater precision.

---

# 4. Self-Regulation

## Core Theme

Discipline, planning, behavioral control, attention management, and consistent follow-through.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organization | Structuring spaces, materials, tasks, or information into usable order. |
| Planning | Preparing future actions or sequences before execution. |
| Prudence | Considering consequences, risks, and long-term costs before acting. |
| Self-Discipline | Acting consistently despite temptation, fatigue, or lack of desire. |
| Persistence | Continuing a task despite obstacles, difficulty, or discouragement. |
| Deliberation | Evaluating options before choosing or acting. |
| Detail Consciousness | Attending carefully to precision, accuracy, and small errors. |
| Reliability | Consistent follow-through on obligations, expectations, and responsibilities. |
| Attention Control | Directing, sustaining, or returning focus despite distraction. |

## Notes

Orderliness and Organization have been consolidated into Organization.

Reliability remains under Self-Regulation. Honor measures the value placed on one's word; Reliability measures the behavioral execution of commitments.

Prudence and Deliberation remain separate. Deliberation concerns the process of considering options. Prudence concerns risk sensitivity and consequence forecasting.

---

# 5. Emotional Stability

## Core Theme

Emotional regulation, resilience, security, recovery, and tolerance of stress or affective disruption.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Emotional Regulation | Managing emotional activation during stress, conflict, or pressure. |
| Emotional Recovery | Returning to baseline after emotional activation. |
| Emotional Threshold | Amount of stimulation, threat, or stress required to trigger emotional activation. |
| Mood Stability | Consistency of mood across time and circumstance. |
| Security | Sense of psychological, relational, social, and future safety. |
| Stress Tolerance | Capacity to remain functional under pressure, strain, or frustration. |
| Emotional Awareness | Noticing and identifying one's own emotional states. |

## Notes

Attachment Security has been consolidated into Security. Attachment security can later appear as a mechanism or expression cluster under Security.

Stress Tolerance and Frustration Tolerance have been consolidated into Stress Tolerance.

Anxiety Regulation has been removed as a separate facet because it is better represented as a specific expression or mechanism within Emotional Regulation.

---

# 6. Exploration

## Core Theme

Curiosity, novelty, imagination, intellectual engagement, complexity tolerance, and discovery through thought or experience.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inquisitiveness | Desire to inquire, investigate, learn, and acquire understanding. |
| Creativity | Generating original, useful, expressive, or unconventional ideas. |
| Imagination | Envisioning possibilities beyond immediate sensory reality. |
| Abstract Thinking | Engaging with theories, patterns, systems, and conceptual ideas. |
| Aesthetic Appreciation | Sensitivity to beauty, art, design, symbolic form, or sensory meaning. |
| Novelty Seeking | Attraction toward new experiences, unfamiliar situations, and change. |
| Need for Cognition | Enjoyment of effortful thought, analysis, and cognitive challenge. |
| Tolerance for Ambiguity | Comfort with uncertainty, unclear meanings, and open-ended problems. |
| Experimentation | Trying new methods, testing possibilities, and learning through variation. |

## Notes

Intellectual Curiosity has been renamed Inquisitiveness for readability and to avoid implying intelligence or cognitive ability.

Discovery Orientation has been removed because its semantic territory overlaps heavily with Inquisitiveness, Novelty Seeking, and Experimentation.

---

# 7. Honesty

## Core Theme

Truthfulness, transparency, reciprocity, principled conduct, and respect for one's word.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Truthfulness | Accuracy of representation and avoidance of falsehood. |
| Transparency | Openness about relevant motives, facts, limits, and intentions. |
| Reciprocity | Respect for mutual exchange, fairness of return, and balanced obligation. |
| Authentic Communication | Communication that reflects genuine belief, intent, and position. |
| Sincerity | Genuineness of motive, presentation, and interpersonal expression. |
| Fairness | Impartiality, equal regard, and resistance to unfair advantage. |
| Honor | Treating commitments, obligations, duties, and one's word as binding principles of conduct. |

## Notes

Honesty is not duplicated as a facet because it is the parent aspect.

Promise Keeping has been removed as a facet because it is better represented as an expression under Honor or Reliability.

---

# 8. Reflection

## Core Theme

Self-awareness, metacognition, perspective-taking, self-evaluation, meaning construction, and revision of beliefs or identity.

## Facets

| Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Introspection | Examining one's own thoughts, feelings, motives, and experiences. |
| Extrospection | Examining others' thoughts, feelings, motives, and behavior. |
| Self-Knowledge | Accurate understanding of one's traits, patterns, motives, and limits. |
| Perspective Taking | Considering viewpoints, experiences, and interpretations beyond one's own. |
| Metacognition | Awareness and regulation of one's own thinking processes. |
| Meaning-Making | Interpreting experiences through significance, purpose, or narrative structure. |
| Identity Coherence | Integrating self-beliefs into a stable but revisable sense of self. |
| Self-Evaluation | Assessing one's behavior, motives, outcomes, and self-concept. |
| Belief Examination | Questioning, testing, or revising beliefs and assumptions. |
| Cognitive Flexibility | Ability to shift interpretation, adapt thought patterns, and consider alternatives. |

## Notes

Reflection was added as an eighth aspect to house introspective, extrospective, metacognitive, and meaning-oriented facets that were not cleanly represented by Exploration alone.

---

# Excluded From Personality Taxonomy

The following constructs are currently excluded from the personality aspect/facet taxonomy because they are better represented elsewhere:

| Construct | Current Handling |
|---|---|
| Spirituality | Identity content or future identity expansion. |
| Traditionalism | Identity/worldview content or future identity expansion. |
| Punitive | Karma model component, especially Relational Equity, Moral Reasoning, and Moral Sensitivity. |
| Hedonism | Removed due to weak fit and semantic ambiguity. |
| Discovery Orientation | Removed due to overlap with Inquisitiveness, Novelty Seeking, and Experimentation. |
| Promise Keeping | Expression under Honor or Reliability rather than facet. |
| Attachment Security | Mechanism/expression cluster under Security rather than separate facet. |
