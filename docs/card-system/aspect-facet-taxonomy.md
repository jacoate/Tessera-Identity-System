## Overwrite Notice

This file replaces the prior eight-aspect draft taxonomy that used Agency, Social Vitality, Communion, Self-Regulation, Emotional Stability, Exploration, Honesty, and Reflection as active aspects.

Reason for replacement:

- the active review model now uses the ten-aspect personality structure currently listed in the README and model manifest;
- Honesty / Honesty-Humility is no longer treated as an active personality aspect or scored bridge;
- moral-integrity content formerly associated with Honesty-Humility is routed to the Karma model, neutral symbol tags, reconciliation context, or source-lineage notes;
- useful older facet material is preserved as candidate facet content where it can improve personality-expression precision without duplicating Karma.

This overwrite does not claim the ten-aspect model is final. It establishes the current working scaffold for review, card drafting, and future digital schema planning.

## Scientific and Speculative Status

This document contains two kinds of information:

1. **Empirical-source-aligned structure**: broad compatibility with established personality research traditions such as Big Five / BFAS-style aspect organization.
2. **Tessera-specific speculative modeling**: candidate facets, card-routing rules, and analog/digital implementation structures that require later validation before being described as psychometric findings.

Empirically supported material should not be overwritten merely for convenience. Speculative Tessera structures may be revised when they improve construct clarity, reduce redundancy, preserve empirical compatibility, or improve practical usability.

## Structural Principle

The current working hierarchy is:

```text
Domain Family
-> Aspect
-> Candidate Facet
-> Expression
-> Reconciliation
```

Domain families are organizational anchors.

Aspects are the primary personality testing units.

Candidate facets describe possible expression channels inside an aspect. They are not final inventories and should be refined through item development, expression drafting, expert review, and eventual empirical testing.

Expressions are observable behavioral evidence for a facet.

Reconciliation explains discrepancies between taxonomy results, contextual response patterns, self-concept, observer reports, and behavioral evidence.

## Candidate Facet Design Rule

A candidate facet should:

- represent a distinct expression pathway within its parent aspect;
- generate multiple observable expressions;
- avoid duplicating another facet or Karma dimension;
- remain narrower than its parent aspect;
- avoid unsupported moral evaluation;
- improve identity-map precision;
- remain open to revision.

Candidate facets are not validated scales unless later evidence supports that status.

## 1. Assertiveness

Core question: How directly does the person express agency, position, direction, or initiative?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direction-Setting | Suggests or establishes a course of action when direction is needed. |
| Initiative | Begins action without external prompting. |
| Verbal Confidence | States preferences, positions, disagreement, or needs directly. |
| Leadership | Coordinates collective activity or organizes group effort. |
| Influence | Shapes attention, decisions, or direction without necessarily holding formal authority. |
| Boundary Assertion | Communicates limits, refusals, or conditions when necessary. |

Preserved older material: Agency, self-efficacy, courage, visibility, perseverance, and achievement-oriented content may inform Assertiveness, Industriousness, or reconciliation cues, but should not recreate the prior Agency aspect without review.

---

## 2. Enthusiasm

Core question: How strongly does the person move toward social energy, shared excitement, and positive engagement?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sociability | Seeks, enjoys, or sustains social company. |
| Expressive Warmth | Shows approachable positive affect and interpersonal receptivity. |
| Positive Activation | Displays excitement, energy, or uplift during engagement. |
| Playful Engagement | Uses humor, spontaneity, lightness, or play in interaction. |
| Social Participation | Joins or contributes to shared social activity. |
| Social Stamina | Maintains engagement across socially demanding environments. |

Preserved older material: Humor, playfulness, affability, personal disclosure, and social stamina remain candidate material, but should be reviewed for redundancy and cultural/contextual bias.

---

## 3. Compassion

Core question: How strongly does the person notice, feel, and respond to others' emotional or practical needs?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Empathic Concern | Experiences concern in response to distress, need, or vulnerability. |
| Responsiveness | Notices and responds to others' emotional or practical states. |
| Nurturance | Offers care, comfort, or support. |
| Protectiveness | Acts to safeguard others from harm or vulnerability. |
| Relational Warmth | Expresses interpersonal softness, welcome, or emotional receptivity. |

Boundary rule: Personality Compassion describes a tendency toward care or responsiveness. Karma evaluates ethical action under cost, tradeoff, harm, power, concealment, or consequence.

---

## 4. Politeness

Core question: How strongly does the person regulate dominance, respect boundaries, and restrain abrasive social behavior?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Respectfulness | Recognizes others' dignity, boundaries, and social standing. |
| Conflict Restraint | Inhibits abrasive, hostile, or unnecessarily escalating behavior. |
| Boundary Respect | Avoids imposing on others without adequate cause or consent. |
| Deference Calibration | Adjusts dominance and deference according to role and context. |
| Non-Intrusiveness | Limits unnecessary pressure, disruption, or overreach. |
| Civility | Maintains courteous conduct during disagreement or tension. |

Boundary rule: Politeness should not be treated as moral goodness. Excessive politeness may also suppress needed assertiveness or accountability.

---

## 5. Industriousness

Core question: How strongly does the person initiate, sustain, and complete effortful goal-directed activity?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Achievement Drive | Pursues accomplishment, mastery, advancement, or excellence. |
| Persistence | Continues effort despite obstacles, fatigue, or discouragement. |
| Task Activation | Starts necessary work without excessive external prompting. |
| Productive Endurance | Sustains effort across longer or more demanding time horizons. |
| Reliability | Follows through on commitments, obligations, or expectations. |
| Attention Control | Directs, sustains, or returns focus despite distraction. |

Preserved older material: Perseverance, reliability, attention control, and self-discipline are retained here as candidate facet material rather than as components of the prior Self-Regulation aspect.

---

## 6. Orderliness

Core question: How strongly does the person prefer structure, sequence, precision, preparation, and environmental organization?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organization | Structures spaces, materials, information, or tasks into usable order. |
| Planning | Prepares sequences, resources, or actions before execution. |
| Precision | Attends to detail, accuracy, and error reduction. |
| Rule Consistency | Prefers stable procedures, standards, or predictable systems. |
| Environmental Order | Maintains orderly physical or digital surroundings. |
| Deliberative Sequencing | Arranges steps before acting when structure is useful. |

Boundary rule: Orderliness is not the same as ethical responsibility. Responsibility under consequence belongs to Karma / Accountability Orientation when morally relevant.

---

## 7. Withdrawal

Core question: How strongly does the person retract, avoid, worry, or become inhibited under perceived threat, uncertainty, or vulnerability?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anxious Anticipation | Expects threat, failure, rejection, danger, or loss. |
| Avoidant Retraction | Pulls away from perceived risk, overload, exposure, or conflict. |
| Self-Consciousness | Monitors the self through imagined or anticipated external judgment. |
| Vulnerability | Feels easily overwhelmed or unprotected under pressure. |
| Ruminative Concern | Replays worry, uncertainty, mistakes, or possible negative outcomes. |
| Security Seeking | Seeks reassurance, certainty, protection, or stable attachment conditions. |

Preserved older material: Security and attachment-relevant content may appear here as mechanisms or expression clusters, but should be reviewed carefully before expanding into object-relations or attachment-specific modules.

---

## 8. Volatility

Core question: How strongly does the person experience rapid emotional escalation, irritability, anger, or affective reactivity?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Irritability | Becomes annoyed, frustrated, or provoked quickly. |
| Anger Reactivity | Responds to perceived obstruction or offense with anger. |
| Emotional Escalation | Emotional intensity rises quickly and may become hard to regulate. |
| Impulsive Expression | Emotion moves rapidly into speech or action. |
| Conflict Activation | Moves toward confrontation when distressed. |
| Recovery Difficulty | Takes longer to return to baseline after activation. |

Boundary rule: Volatility is not a moral defect. Karma becomes relevant only when emotional activation intersects with harm, accountability, coercion, intent, or consequence.

## 9. Openness

Core question: How strongly does the person respond to novelty, aesthetics, imagination, symbolism, emotional depth, or experiential possibility?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aesthetic Sensitivity | Responds to beauty, art, design, sound, symbol, or form. |
| Imagination | Generates inner imagery, possibility, fantasy, or symbolic alternatives. |
| Emotional Depth | Experiences nuanced, layered, or meaning-rich inner states. |
| Novelty Receptivity | Welcomes unfamiliar experiences, perspectives, or environments. |
| Meaning-Making | Interprets experience through significance, narrative, or symbolic pattern. |
| Experimentation | Tests possibilities through variation, play, or experiential exploration. |

Preserved older material: Creativity and tolerance for ambiguity may be routed here, but should be kept distinct from Intellect-oriented abstraction.

## 10. Intellect

Core question: How strongly does the person engage abstract reasoning, conceptual exploration, analysis, and problem-structuring?

Candidate facets:

| Candidate Facet | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Analytical Curiosity | Enjoys questioning, explanation, investigation, and reasoning. |
| Abstract Thinking | Engages theories, principles, systems, patterns, or conceptual models. |
| Need for Cognition | Finds effortful thinking, analysis, or cognitive challenge rewarding. |
| Problem Structuring | Breaks complex issues into solvable or interpretable parts. |
| Belief Examination | Tests, questions, or revises assumptions and interpretations. |
| Cognitive Flexibility | Shifts frames, considers alternatives, and adapts interpretations. |

Preserved older material: Reflection-related facets such as introspection, metacognition, identity coherence, and self-evaluation are important, but may be better handled as reconciliation, identity-map, development, or future self/identity modules rather than as a separate personality aspect without further review.
