# Provisional Primary Aspect Set

## Status

This document records the current review-ready aspect set for the Tessera personality taxonomy. It replaces earlier language that treated the previous taxonomy as locked or fixed.

The aspect set remains provisional and subject to revision. Changes should follow the repository revision and overwrite policy.

## Purpose of Aspects

Aspects are the primary personality testing units in the current analog taxonomy model.

An aspect should describe a broad dispositional tendency that can later be clarified through facets, expressions, symbols, and reconciliation.

Aspect questions should ask what pattern of disposition appears, how consistently it appears, and under which conditions it becomes direct, inhibited, contextual, or conflicted.

Aspects should not directly score moral character. Moral-context interpretation belongs to the Karma model.

## Aspect Definitions

### Assertiveness

Measures the tendency to express agency, direction, position, initiative, or interpersonal directiveness.

Primary question: how directly does the person express agency, position, direction, or initiative?

### Enthusiasm

Measures the tendency toward social energy, shared excitement, positive engagement, and approach-oriented participation.

Primary question: how strongly does the person move toward social energy, shared excitement, and positive engagement?

### Compassion

Measures the tendency to notice, feel, and respond to others' emotional or practical needs.

Primary question: how strongly does the person notice and respond to others' distress, vulnerability, or need?

### Politeness

Measures the tendency to regulate dominance, respect boundaries, and restrain abrasive or imposing social behavior.

Primary question: how strongly does the person preserve respect, restraint, and social boundaries?

### Industriousness

Measures the tendency to initiate, sustain, and complete effortful goal-directed activity.

Primary question: how strongly does the person begin, persist in, and complete difficult or effortful work?

### Orderliness

Measures the tendency toward structure, sequence, preparation, precision, organization, and environmental arrangement.

Primary question: how strongly does the person prefer ordered, planned, precise, or structured conditions?

### Withdrawal

Measures the tendency toward retraction, avoidance, inhibition, worry, or threat-sensitive withdrawal under uncertainty, vulnerability, or perceived risk.

Primary question: how strongly does the person pull back, avoid, or become inhibited under perceived threat or uncertainty?

### Volatility

Measures the tendency toward rapid emotional escalation, irritability, anger, frustration, or affective reactivity.

Primary question: how strongly does the person experience and express reactive emotional escalation?

### Openness

Measures the tendency toward novelty, aesthetics, imagination, symbolism, emotional depth, experiential possibility, and meaning-making.

Primary question: how strongly does the person respond to imagination, beauty, novelty, symbols, and experiential possibility?

### Intellect

Measures the tendency toward abstract reasoning, conceptual exploration, analysis, curiosity, and problem structuring.

Primary question: how strongly does the person engage ideas, reasoning, systems, and conceptual complexity?

## Honesty-Humility Status

Honesty-Humility is not currently retained as an active personality domain, aspect, or scored bridge.

Its moral content is currently treated as better represented by the Karma model, especially through dimensions corresponding to:

- sincerity / authentic presentation;
- fairness / relational equity;
- modesty / self-regard calibration;
- greed-avoidance / material orientation.

Honesty-Humility-derived content may still be used as source material when it contributes nonredundant function, including:

- neutral question wording;
- symbol tags;
- reconciliation references;
- Karma source-lineage notes;
- manual commentary.

It should not become a separate score unless later review identifies a distinct nonredundant behavioral function not represented by the taxonomy, Karma model, symbols, or reconciliation process.

## Compassion Boundary Note

Compassion appears in both personality and moral-context discussions and must be kept functionally distinct.

In the personality taxonomy, Compassion refers to general responsiveness to others' needs or distress.

In the Karma model, compassion-related interpretation concerns ethical action under morally relevant conditions such as cost, harm, power, concealment, accountability, or consequence.

A personality response should not by itself be treated as proof of moral character.
