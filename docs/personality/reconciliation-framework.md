# Reconciliation Framework

Version: 0.2 Provisional
Status: Active / Provisional
Scope: Tessera personality reconciliation, symbol reference, and Karma-context review

---

## Overwrite Notice

This file updates the earlier reconciliation framework to include neutral symbols, candidate facets, response-pattern markers, and possible Karma review context.

Reason for replacement:

- reconciliation now receives information from response patterns, symbols, expression validation, and possible Karma review contexts;
- contradictions should remain informative rather than being treated as automatic errors;
- future digital infrastructure needs a clear difference between reconciliation hypotheses and confirmed explanations.

---

## Purpose

Reconciliation explains discrepancies between assessment outcomes, self-concept, observer reports, expression evidence, and contextual behavior.

It helps answer:

```text
Why might this tendency appear in one place and not another?
```

Reconciliation does not automatically prove error, deception, pathology, or moral failure.

---

## Principle

Contradictions are treated as informative data rather than simple error.

A mismatch may suggest context sensitivity, role effects, social inhibition, self-concept conflict, limited opportunity, developmental change, or measurement uncertainty.

---

## Reconciliation Inputs

Reconciliation may draw from:

- aspect response patterns;
- derived consistency/contextuality markers;
- candidate facet routing;
- expression validation;
- neutral symbol themes;
- possible Karma review contexts;
- participant reflection;
- observer evidence;
- setting or role information.

---

## Reconciliation Categories

Current provisional categories include:

| Category | Working Meaning |
|---|---|
| Situational Restriction | Trait may exist, but environment limits expression. |
| Social Inhibition | Expression may be suppressed by judgment, conflict, or rejection concerns. |
| Selective Expression | Tendency appears only in particular contexts, roles, or relationships. |
| Role Suppression | A role or institution discourages expression. |
| Opportunity Deficit | The person has not had enough opportunity to express or observe the tendency. |
| Self-Concept Mismatch | The person's self-image and response pattern diverge. |
| Adaptive Masking | Expression is strategically modified for safety, belonging, or role navigation. |
| Contextual Cost Sensitivity | Expression decreases when cost, status risk, or social consequence rises. |
| Expression Integrity Review | The tendency appears active, but implementation may be distorted or incoherent. |
| Measurement Uncertainty | Available evidence is insufficient or inconsistent. |

---

## Symbol Use

Symbols may help identify the kind of context involved in a mismatch.

Example:

```text
High Assertiveness pattern
+ low authority-facing expression
+ Status / Cost / Agency symbols
-> possible status-risk inhibition or role suppression review
```

Symbols do not create hidden scores and should not be treated as proof of motive.

---

## Karma Use

Karma may provide reconciliation context when expression intersects with ethical stakes such as harm, exploitation, accountability, autonomy, concealment, or cost.

Possible Karma context should not be treated as a confirmed moral outcome. It should route review to relevant questions.

---

## Outcome

Reconciliation supports identity calibration, self-understanding, and clearer distinction between:

```text
trait tendency
contextual expression
observed behavior
ethical implementation
self-concept narrative
measurement uncertainty
```
