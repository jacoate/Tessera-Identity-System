# Response Sequence and Repeated-Review Methodology

Version: 0.2 Provisional
Status: Active / Provisional
Scope: Tessera aspect assessment procedure

---

## Overwrite Notice

This file replaces the prior **Three-Pass Assessment Methodology** framing that could be read as a required procedure.

Reason for replacement:

- repeated assessment remains valuable, but should not be hard-coded as mandatory before prototype testing;
- the current model derives consistency and contextuality from the final sequence and pattern of responses;
- analog usability requires flexible procedures that can scale from short card sessions to deeper review sessions.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of repeated review is to improve interpretive confidence while preserving meaningful variance.

Variance should not automatically be treated as error. It may suggest contextual expression, identity conflict, role modulation, social inhibition, uncertainty, or a need for further expression validation.

---

## Core Procedure

```text
Aspect Card Sort
-> Response Pattern Review
-> Strength Estimate
-> Consistency / Contextuality Review
-> Candidate Facet Routing
-> Reconciliation Cue Selection
```

The participant does not directly choose the consistency marker. The marker is inferred from response patterns and then may be discussed or annotated through reflection.

---

## Optional Repeated Review Modes

### Mode A: Single-Session Pattern Review

A single aspect deck is sorted once. The final distribution is reviewed for strength, consistency, contextuality, and contradiction.

Best for:

- short tabletop use;
- early prototype play;
- rapid identity-map construction.

### Mode B: Alternate-Wording Review

A later review uses alternate item wording or reshuffled cards to examine whether the pattern remains similar.

Best for:

- reducing memorization effects;
- comparing first impression against later reflection;
- detecting item-wording sensitivity.

### Mode C: Context-Pressure Review

A follow-up review emphasizes context, cost, authority, role, visibility, social pressure, or uncertainty.

Best for:

- identifying suppression conditions;
- routing reconciliation cards;
- distinguishing trait absence from inhibited expression.

## Derived Result Qualities

| Derived Quality | Pattern That May Suggest It |
|---|---|
| Stable | Similar direction across core, behavioral, inhibition, and context items. |
| Contextual | High or clear tendency in some item types, but reduced expression under specific conditions. |
| Conflicted | Endorsement of a tendency alongside strong suppression, avoidance, or reverse-pattern evidence. |
| Uncertain | Mixed responses without a clear directional or contextual pattern. |
| Aspirational | Requires participant reflection indicating a gap between desired self and typical behavior. |

These are interpretive markers, not absolute classifications.

---

## Interpretation Language

Use:

- may suggest;
- appears consistent with;
- may reflect;
- provides possible context for;
- should be reviewed alongside.

Avoid:

- proves;
- guarantees;
- diagnoses;
- always means;
- never means.

---

## Digital Expansion Note

A future app may automate pattern review, but should preserve raw response distributions, item types, and contextual tags. Automated labels should remain interpretable and reviewable rather than being treated as final truth.
