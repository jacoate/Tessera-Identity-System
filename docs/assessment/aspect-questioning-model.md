# Aspect Questioning Model

## Status

This document describes the current provisional questioning model for aspect assessment decks.

It replaces earlier framing that treated repeated assessment, marker selection, or three-pass procedure as fixed requirements. The model remains open to revision.

## Purpose

Aspect questions are designed to estimate broad dispositional patterns through analog card sorting.

An aspect deck should help identify:

- aspect strength;
- behavioral consistency;
- contextual directiveness;
- inhibition or suppression patterns;
- possible reconciliation cues;
- symbol themes that may become relevant later.

Aspect questions should not directly test Karma, moral worth, or fixed identity type.

## Question-Family Structure

Aspect decks should use functionally varied question families rather than only repeated paraphrases.

Recommended item types:

1. **Core tendency**: broad disposition.
2. **Motivation / preference**: what feels engaging, draining, natural, or preferred.
3. **Behavioral tendency**: recurring action pattern.
4. **Threshold / activation**: when the aspect becomes active.
5. **Inhibition / suppression**: what blocks or reduces expression.
6. **Context variation**: how the aspect shifts across roles, audiences, settings, or consequences.
7. **Reverse / contrast items**: checks against overgeneralized endorsement.

## Response Sort

Recommended analog response zones:

```text
Strongly Not Me
Mostly Not Me
Mixed / Depends
Mostly Me
Strongly Me
```

The final result should be based on the full sequence and pattern of card placement rather than isolated responses.

## Derived Markers

Markers should be derived from the completed response pattern. Participants should not self-certify stability, contextuality, or conflict as the primary scoring source.

Participant reflection may be recorded after the pattern is reviewed, but it should not replace the derived result.

Preferred marker language:

- the response pattern suggests;
- the sequence appears consistent with;
- this may indicate;
- this pattern may reflect.

Avoid absolute or diagnostic phrasing.

## Provisional Marker Categories

### Stable

The aspect appears similarly across core tendency, behavioral tendency, threshold, inhibition, context, and reverse/contrast items.

Suggested by:

- consistent high or low placement across item types;
- low contradiction between core tendency and behavioral expression;
- limited context-specific suppression.

### Contextual

The aspect appears in some settings but shifts under specific social, emotional, authority, role, cost, or audience conditions.

Suggested by:

- high or moderate core tendency;
- mixed or low context-pressure items;
- clear variation across role, audience, authority, or consequence conditions.

### Conflicted

The person appears to identify with or endorse the aspect while also endorsing suppression, hesitation, avoidance, reversal, or contradiction.

Suggested by:

- high tendency plus high inhibition;
- high preference plus low behavioral expression;
- strong endorsement of both direct and suppressive items.

### Uncertain

The pattern is too scattered, mixed, or weakly directional to support a confident interpretation.

Suggested by:

- many Mixed / Depends placements;
- no clear item-type trend;
- inconsistent response pattern without an interpretable contextual theme.

### Aspirational

The person identifies the aspect as desired, idealized, or self-relevant but not consistently expressed behaviorally.

This marker should generally require explicit participant reflection or comparison between actual behavior and desired self-image. It should not be inferred from card placement alone unless the deck contains specific actual-vs-ideal item structures.

## Behavioral Consistency and Directiveness Chart

The instruction manual should include a chart that maps response patterns to consistency and directiveness.

Example chart logic:

```text
High core tendency + high behavior + low inhibition
→ pattern appears consistent / direct

High core tendency + high behavior + high context variation
→ pattern appears present but contextual

High core tendency + low behavior + high inhibition
→ pattern appears conflicted or suppressed

Mixed core tendency + mixed behavior + mixed context variation
→ pattern appears uncertain; facet or expression validation may be needed
```

This chart should not be framed as definitive diagnosis. It is an analog interpretive aid.

## Neutral Symbol Tags

Aspect cards may include neutral contextual symbols when the card theme may later help reconciliation or Karma interpretation.

Symbols are non-scoring. They do not modify the aspect score and do not prove moral meaning.

A symbol indicates that the card contains a theme that may later provide context.

For example, an Assertiveness card involving public leadership might carry Agency and Status symbols. That does not mean the participant is controlling or status-seeking. It means that later conflicts involving authority, credit, visibility, or social position may reference that card during reconciliation.

## Example Aspect Card Structure

Player-facing fields:

```text
Aspect: Assertiveness
Question: When a group is uncertain about what to do, I am likely to suggest a direction.
Response Sort: Strongly Not Me / Mostly Not Me / Mixed / Mostly Me / Strongly Me
Symbols: Agency, Status
```

Designer/manual reference fields:

```text
Item Type: Behavioral tendency
Facet Lean: Leadership / Initiative
Possible Reconciliation Cue: role suppression, status-risk sensitivity, social inhibition
Karma Relevance: none unless later activated by scenario
```

## Analog Constraint

The system should remain practical with physical cards, printed symbols, sorting zones, reference charts, and manual interpretation.

No question model should require a digital database, hidden automated calculation, or software utility to function.
