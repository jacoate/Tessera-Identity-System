# Context Symbol Layer

## Status

This document defines the current provisional role of neutral symbols in the Tessera analog card system.

Symbols are not a scored bridge between the personality taxonomy and the Karma model. They are non-scoring contextual markers that may help interpretation, reconciliation, and cross-reference.

## Purpose

Symbols identify themes that may become relevant when a later card, contradiction, or Karma scenario activates a related context.

A symbol does not say what the participant is. It marks what the card is about.

For example:

```text
A card about speaking up under public scrutiny may carry Status and Cost symbols.
```

This does not mean the participant is status-seeking, cowardly, brave, or morally compromised. It means the response may later provide context if a reconciliation or Karma scenario involves recognition, public judgment, social risk, or cost.

## Non-Scoring Rule

Symbols do not:

- modify personality scores;
- create Karma scores;
- prove intent;
- diagnose motivation;
- accumulate into hidden traits;
- determine moral interpretation;
- replace reconciliation.

Symbols may:

- help route attention during reconciliation;
- identify repeated context themes;
- support manual cross-reference;
- clarify whether a contradiction is related to role, status, cost, care, agency, disclosure, accountability, equity, or resource context;
- help connect a personality response to a later Karma scenario without converting it into Karma evidence by itself.

## Provisional Symbol Set

The symbol set should remain compact for analog usability. The current working target is 6 to 10 active symbols, with 8 as the preferred prototype size.

Current provisional symbols:

| Symbol | Descriptive Theme |
|---|---|
| Disclosure | truth, concealment, transparency, masking |
| Equity | fairness, reciprocity, exploitation, unequal exchange |
| Status | prestige, recognition, superiority, credit, image |
| Resource | gain, scarcity, possession, material reward |
| Care | harm, need, compassion, protection, responsiveness |
| Agency | control, autonomy, initiative, coercion, direction |
| Accountability | blame, repair, responsibility, excuses, ownership |
| Cost | sacrifice, risk, discomfort, courage, consequence |

This list is provisional and should be revised if symbols become redundant, too broad, too numerous, or difficult to use physically.

## Relationship to Honesty-Humility

Honesty-Humility is not retained as an active personality domain or scored bridge in the current taxonomy. Its useful nonredundant content may be decomposed into symbol themes where appropriate.

Examples:

- sincerity themes may appear as Disclosure;
- fairness themes may appear as Equity;
- modesty or image themes may appear as Status;
- greed-avoidance or material appetite themes may appear as Resource.

These symbols remain neutral. They do not recreate Honesty-Humility as a hidden score.

## Reconciliation Use

Symbols can help identify what kind of mismatch may be present.

Example:

```text
Aspect result: high Assertiveness
Expression result: low public disagreement
Repeated symbols: Status, Cost, Agency
```

Possible reconciliation interpretation:

```text
The pattern may reflect context-sensitive assertiveness, status-risk sensitivity, cost sensitivity, role suppression, or selective expression rather than low assertiveness itself.
```

The symbol points to a context to investigate. It does not determine the explanation.

## Karma Use

A personality card symbol may become relevant if a later Karma scenario activates the same theme.

Example:

```text
Personality card symbols: Agency, Status
Later Karma scenario: taking or refusing public credit for a group outcome
```

The earlier symbol pattern may provide context, but the Karma scenario must still be interpreted based on the scenario response, tradeoff, consequence, intent, accountability, and reconciliation review.

## Manual Language

Suggested manual wording:

```text
Symbols are neutral contextual markers printed on selected cards. They identify themes that may become relevant during reconciliation or Karma review. Symbols do not modify personality scores, create moral judgments, prove intent, or accumulate into hidden traits. Their function is referential: they help players and interpreters notice when separate card responses share a common contextual theme.
```
