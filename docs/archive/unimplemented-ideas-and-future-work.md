## 3. Broader Self / Identity Theory Notes

Earlier theory notes emphasized that identity is dynamically constructed in context.

Useful preserved concepts:

1. People experience identity as stable while self-content shifts with context.
2. Self-concepts organize perception, attention, goals, evaluations, and possible selves.
3. Identities are meaning frameworks that guide behavior.
4. Context can activate different identities.
5. Behavior follows the currently active identity more than a globally fixed self-description.
6. Identity activation changes attention, memory, interpretation, motivation, and persistence.
7. Thinking serves action.
8. Self and identity are social products.
9. Reflexive and reflective processing both influence identity expression.
10. Social comparison and inclusion/exclusion processes alter self-evaluation.


The token economy is not yet implemented.

The token model is expected to govern all testing steps and will determine the properties of test methodology.

Earlier rough draft:

```text

10 tokens

```

Current unresolved decisions:

1. Total number of options per scenario.
2. Number of additive options.
3. Number of aversive options.
4. Token pool size.
5. Whether all tokens must be allocated.
6. Maximum tokens per option.
7. Whether only one option can receive the maximum.
8. Whether inverse options subtract from trait score, expression integrity, or both.
9. Whether inverse selections trigger Karma flags.
10. Whether a separate Reconciliation trigger score is needed.

Important correction archived from discussion:

The system should not penalize a person for selecting too many favorable options.

Reason:

There is no legitimate empirical unit for deciding when a favorable or not-bad trait presentation becomes too good.

A favorability saturation penalty risks introducing hidden social-engineered bias.

Replace this rejected idea:

```text
Favorability Saturation Penalty
```

with:

```text
Identity-Attractive Aversive Option Design
```

and:

```text
Reconciliation Triggering
```

The system should detect malformed mechanisms, not punish favorable self-presentation by default.

## 6. Identity-Attractive Aversive Option Design

This idea is important but not yet implemented.

Core concept:

Aversive options should not be obvious bad answers. They should be written as behaviors that feel appealing to a desired identity while encoding a distorted mechanism.

The goal is not to trick the evaluator maliciously.

The goal is to model how distorted behavior often appears in real life:

```text
surface-valid motive
+
maladaptive or ethically distorted mechanism
```

Recommended option syntax:

```text
Observer-readable behavior + contextual pressure + surface-valid motive
```

Examples:

Functional leadership option:

```text
Clarifies the group's goal so everyone can coordinate under time pressure.
```

Aversive leadership-coded option:

```text
Takes over the plan so the group stops slowing things down.
```

Surface appeal:

```text
Decisiveness
Efficiency
Leadership
```

Latent mechanism:

```text
Dominance
Low cooperation
Poor deliberation
Possible relational inequity
```

Functional compassion option:

```text
Asks what support would be useful before deciding how to help.
```

Aversive compassion-coded option:

```text
Steps in to solve the person's problem before asking what they want.
```

Surface appeal:

```text
Helpfulness
Care
Responsiveness
```

Latent mechanism:

```text
Paternalism
Autonomy violation
Low prudence
Low perspective-taking
```

Future implementation should distinguish between:

1. Low expression
2. Functional expression
3. Aversive or malformed expression

## 7. Future Scoring Architecture Ideas

A simple positive-minus-negative scoring model is probably insufficient.

Preserved future scoring idea:

Use separate score layers:

1. Trait Activation Score
2. Expression Integrity Score
3. Karma Shadow Flag
4. Reconciliation Trigger

Example:

```text
A person dominates a group to force action.
```

Possible interpretation:

```text
Agency activation: high
Leadership activation: high
Expression integrity: low
Karma shadow: Relational Equity / Moral Sensitivity
Reconciliation: Desired-Self Leadership vs coercive implementation
```

This protects against the mistake of treating distorted expression as absence of the trait.

Aversive expression may reveal that the trait exists but is poorly calibrated or ethically distorted.
